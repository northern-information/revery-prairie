# Revery Prairie — Design

## Core Premise

Revery Prairie is a nonviolent ecological roguelike about stewardship, uncertainty, and deep time.

The player explores ancient prairie ruins to recover seeds, plants, pollinators, fungal strains, and dormant genetic possibilities. These are returned to the surface prairie, where they are planted, bred, nurtured, and observed across evolving seasonal cycles.

The game is not about conquest, optimization, extraction, or infinite growth.

It is about:

- intervention
- relinquishment
- consequence
- adaptation
- coexistence
- ecological memory

The prairie is not controlled by the player. It is influenced by them.

---

## Core Loops

### Small Loop — Ruin Expedition

The player:

- explores procedural prairie ruins
- solves environmental and symbolic puzzles
- recovers biological possibilities
- returns to the surface prairie

Recovered materials may include:

- seeds
- dormant plants
- pollinators
- fungi
- invasive specimens
- genetic fragments
- ecological records
- extinct lineages

Ruins are not “loot dungeons.”
They are memory structures within deep time.

---

### Medium Loop — Ecological Stewardship

On the prairie surface, the player:

- plants species
- crossbreeds lineages
- studies inheritance
- manages burns
- encourages pollinator routes
- responds to invasive pressure
- shapes ecological relationships

The player nudges conditions rather than issuing total control.

Ecology is:

- probabilistic
- emergent
- partially unknowable
- nonlinear
- seasonal
- relational

The prairie responds according to interacting systems, not deterministic formulas.

---

### Big Loop — The Revery

The primary long-form cycle is the Revery.

After preparing the prairie, the player gradually relinquishes direct control as ecological momentum builds. Time accelerates. Seasons emerge naturally through environmental thresholds rather than discrete “turns.”

The player then witnesses:

- growth
- collapse
- migration
- fire
- dormancy
- mutation
- invasive spread
- symbiosis
- extinction
- recovery

The Revery is not a cutscene or idle timer.
It is the game’s central act of surrender and observation.

The player watches deep time unfold.

---

## Time & Seasons

Time is phenological rather than calendar-based.

Seasons emerge from accumulated ecological conditions:

- thaw
- moisture
- flowering
- pollinator activity
- drought stress
- seed hardening
- dormancy

There is no hard:

> “End Turn → Begin Summer”

The prairie transitions gradually and ambiguously.

The player may act at their own pace during active ecological periods.

Eventually, ecological systems acquire enough inertia that the prairie “stops waiting,” initiating the Revery phase.

---

## Design Philosophy

### Anti-Optimization

The game resists:

- productivity pressure
- throughput gameplay
- deterministic builds
- resource extraction fantasies
- infinite scaling
- solved metas

There is no “best build.”
Context determines ecological value.

A fragile species in one Revery may become foundational in another.

Over-optimization can create ecological fragility:

- monocultures
- pollinator collapse
- blight vulnerability
- invasive susceptibility

---

### Anti-Capitalist Structure

The player is not an owner or conqueror.

They are:

- steward
- participant
- observer
- temporary influence

The prairie is alive independent of player productivity.

The game values:

- resilience
- coexistence
- biodiversity
- adaptation
- memory
- ambiguity
- observation
- reverence

---

## Genetics & Inheritance

Genetics are partially legible but never fully transparent.

Players can observe:

- lineage
- inheritance
- dominance
- mutation
- stress events
- visible traits
- ecological tendencies

But outcomes remain probabilistic and emergent.

The goal is:

- folk ecology
- intuition
- observation
- experimentation
- mythology

... not spreadsheet optimization.

Players should gradually become naturalists rather than engineers.

---

## Ecological Knowledge

The prairie should produce:

- suspected traits
- observed behaviors
- incomplete understanding
- inherited tendencies
- seasonal memory

Examples:

- “Late blooming”
- “Cold tolerant”
- “Moth favored”
- “Fire hungry”
- “Prone to collapse after drought”

The player develops ecological wisdom through repeated Reveries.

---

## Failure

Failure is transformative, not punitive.

Collapse does not mean:

> “Game Over.”

Instead, the prairie enters new states:

- ash prairie
- fungal prairie
- invasive bloom
- nocturnal prairie
- migratory prairie
- hybrid prairie
- silent prairie

The world continues becoming.

Failure should be melancholic, beautiful, and informative.

---

## The Invasive Biome

An evolving alien invasive biome spreads alongside the prairie ecosystem.

It is not evil. It is just another ecosystem trying to survive. It should also be:

- beautiful
- adaptive
- intelligent
- seductive
- ecologically compelling

Possible long-form outcomes:

1. Prairie continuity
2. Invasive transformation
3. Ecological symbiosis

No ending represents total domination.

---

## Emotional Tone

The game should evoke:

- deep time
- prairie solitude
- ecological reverence
- seasonal melancholy
- memory
- wind
- dormancy
- emergence
- surrender

The emotional rhythm is:

1. Exploration
2. Intervention
3. Relinquishment
4. Observation
5. Interpretation
6. Adaptation

The player is not mastering the prairie.

They are learning how to listen to it.

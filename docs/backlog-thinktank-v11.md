# Backlog thinktank — v11

A continuation of v10. v10 ran 2 rounds, locking the holographic day/night phase (four days and four nights per year, derived from `seasonalPhase`) and the price-it-don't-monetize-it doctrine for the artifact (one-time purchase, DRM-free, demo bounded to the first tenure). v11 opens with a question Tyler brought to the room on 2026-05-24: _do we need a concept of an equipped item now that meteorite hallowed ground (`RP-18`) and the field camera (`RP-23`) are in flight?_ The seed observation: the existing "[x] to drop" + drag-and-drop pipeline is vestigial from when the game was crafting-heavy, and the verb is wrong — you don't _drop_ a field camera, you _set it up_; you don't _drop_ a meteorite to make a sacred space, you _place_ it. The room found that v4 R7's _the inventory is the character sheet_ has been waiting for exactly this mechanic — the seam where carrying becomes meaning.

What follows is the rounds. Decisions are stated as decisions. Open questions are flagged. v11 is _additive_ to v10 — it does not invalidate v10 unless a round here explicitly amends it. Items that should propagate back to earlier versions are tagged `**Amendment to v{N}:**`.

## Open and locked

**Locked in round 1:**

- **Two verbs, not one.** `[x] to drop` is preserved untouched as a general inventory affordance for every item type. _Place_ is added as a second, distinct verb for placeable items. The steward chooses which gesture they mean.
- **New field on `GameState`: `equippedItemUid: ItemUid | null`.** Single-owner write through the inventory layer, registered in `EXPECTED_FIELDS`, uid-keyed for stack/sort/merge resilience like `state.glintingCoins` and `state.seedGenomes`.
- **Left-click (currently unused outside cursor positioning) becomes the place action when something is in hand.** Nothing-in-hand left-click behavior is preserved.
- **Drag stays for in-grid rearrangement.** Drag-from-grid-to-world is retired as the _placement_ path for placeable items; drop via `[x]` remains for those items.
- **Each placeable item registers a `PlaceableSpec`** — `{ verb, canPlace, place }`. Hover hints and in-game prose read the item's verb (_set_, _set up_, _lay_, _place_, _sow_), not a uniform substrate.
- **In-hand is sticky.** Each place consumes one; the stack decrements; in-hand auto-clears when empty. An explicit unequip path is required (right-click or a small keybinding, deferred to spec).
- **Loaded cursor.** The cursor's hot-pink highlight tile is unchanged in form. When something is in hand and the hovered tile passes `canPlace`, the cursor's inner glyph reads as the in-hand item. When `canPlace` is false, the loaded glyph drops out; the cursor returns to its plain highlight. Hot-pink-as-user-action stays locked.
- **Permacomputer HUD affordance.** A slot on the bottom-bar instrument cluster (`RP-28`) shows the in-hand item's glyph and name in hot pink. The bag holds; the hand declares.
- **The noun in player-facing copy is _in hand_, not _equipped_.** Code can keep `equippedItemUid` for engineering clarity; manual entries, hover hints, and prose read "in hand," "take in hand," "set down."
- **Amendment to v4 R7:** _the inventory is the character sheet_ now has its mechanic. The in-hand-and-place layer is the seam where that doctrine becomes a verb instead of a metaphor.

**Locked in round 2:**

- **The prairie speaks one visual language.** ASCII-on-canvas for the world, sparse UI affordances (dialog box, permacomputer, inventory grid) in their existing register, hot-pink reserved for steward-actions. No character has an animated portrait. Characters land through voice — dialog, music cue, glyph color.
- **Gron's `/gron.gif` is retired.** The asset is inheritance from a previous project, not a design choice. The `portrait?` field on `CharacterEntry` / `CharacterDefinition` is removed; `DialogBox` no longer renders portraits.
- **Emily is the threshold figure**, not Gron. Her authority is her stillness and her words ("you will return before the winter solstice, to revery"). The cosmology does not need a face for the first character.
- **`Gate` is flagged as not-a-character.** The locked-gate interactable wearing a `CharacterEntry` is the system telling us the `Character` type is overloaded. Deferred to its own round/spec — does not block the portrait retirement.

**Locked in round 3:**

- **Cliffs are not a tile type — elevation is the substrate, the threshold is the verb.** RP-41 is implemented by extending the existing `state.elevation` field (raise the genesis amplitude on `UpliftEpoch`) and gating movement on an `isClimbableStep(state, fromKey, toKey, threshold)` predicate that returns false when adjacent elevation deltas exceed the threshold. No new tile, no new glyph — the iso lift in `projection.ts` already does the 3D. **Amends the v7 round's framing of RP-41.**
- **The prairie does not owe the steward access.** Post-genesis, a connectivity pass from the spawn tile computes `state.reachableMass: Set<string>`. Tiles outside the mass remain part of the world — visible, lit, weathered, unwalkable. No ramp-carving to force full reachability. The unreachable cohort is the feature; mesas-you-can-see-but-not-touch are how the prairie expresses scale.
- **The cliff is a shadow, not a tile.** Renderer draws a dark cliff-face line on the iso side wall between adjacent tiles whose elevation delta exceeds the threshold — an extension to existing tile composition, not a new pass. One visual language (v11 R2) preserved.
- **X-ray, generalized.** The v7-round x-ray idea is recast as an alpha rule applied to any lifted tile whose iso projection would occlude the avatar's bounding box. No longer a cliff-tile property.
- **Amendment to v7 R3 (RP-41):** the framing of cliffs as an impassable tile type with stacked glyphs and arches is superseded. The implementation is elevation amplitude + walkable-step threshold + connectivity bookkeeping.

**Locked in round 4:**

- **The camera notices change, not events.** Capture is diff-driven — a single sim-loop hook at flora-lifecycle cadence appends a new `TimeLapseFrame` iff any of the 9 captured cells differs from the last frame on the roll. `recordCameraSubjectEvent`, its seven or eight site-specific call sites, the `CameraSubject` use in the camera pipeline, and the `subject` field on `TimeLapseFrame` retire. A baseline frame is captured at placement.
- **The photograph is the captured 3x3, iso-projected.** Playback renders the captured cells using the same iso transform `renderer.ts` uses for the world, at a smaller cell-pitch, framed with the existing `film-grain-overlay` class. The `GelBandView` exits primary playback and is preserved for the scan instrument.
- **The roll is a contact sheet.** Playback is a grid of all frames at once, chronological. Variance is itself the read — sparse roll says quiet patch, dense roll says lively one. Click-to-expand on a single frame is the held-read affordance; cross-fade lives in the expanded view only.
- **Film count = captured changes.** One detected change consumes one film unit, tightening the `RP-15` wear coupling.
- **The camera stays silent.** No instrument voice added to review.
- **Amendment to `RP-23` and `RP-24`.** Both inherit the new pipeline. Predecessor footage from `RP-24` uses a `degradation` prop (`{ grain, tint, glyphLeak }`) on the photograph component; current-tenure frames pass zero, predecessor frames pass non-zero. Contact-sheet density itself becomes a register the steward reads when encountering a predecessor's camera.

## Round 1: to drop, and to place

_Tyler: "i think we might need a concept of 'equipped item' now. im working on meteorite hallowed ground and field camera. '[x] to drop' and drag and drop feels very unsatisfying. that mechanic/language is vestigial from when this was more of a crafting game with lots of inventory management. and truthfully you don't 'drop' a field camera. you set it up. and you don't 'drop' a meteorite to make a sacred space. you place it. gaming it out a bit, i think if you had a bunch of meteorites in your inventory you could equip any one of them and then probably left click (currently unused) would place them one at a time. you would need to equip each one individually."_

> **Astrid:** The verb is the relationship. To _drop_ a stone is to disown it — to let go of something you no longer want. To _place_ a stone is to dedicate it — to declare a position the stone is now to hold. The prairie's whole substrate has wanted the second verb for a long time; the crafting-game language is vestigial in exactly the way Tyler says, presuming the inventory was a buffer and the world was the destination for surplus. The prairie's inventory is not a buffer; it is what the steward is carrying, and the carrying matters. _Dropping_ is allowed — the steward should be free to disown a thing, the bag should not be a cage — but it must not be the _only_ verb. v4 R7 named the inventory the character sheet; this round is when that line earns a second verb. Each placeable item declares the sentence the steward is finishing — the meteorite is _set_ or _laid_; the camera is _set up_; a seed, when this lands on it, is _sown_. The drop is still there for the steward who just wants to put a thing down. The place is there for the steward who wants to _mean_ something by putting it down. _One verb is a warehouse. Two verbs is a relationship._

> **Boon:** Substrate. New field on `GameState`: `equippedItemUid: ItemUid | null`. Single-owner write through the inventory layer, registered in `EXPECTED_FIELDS`. uid-keyed for the same reason `state.glintingCoins` and `state.seedGenomes` are — survives `autoSort`, merge, stack, and split without losing the reference. Left-click, currently routed only to cursor positioning, becomes the place action when something is in hand; otherwise left-click stays cursor-only. The `[x] to drop` keymap is preserved untouched — drop remains a general inventory affordance for every item type, placeables included. What goes away is drag-from-grid-to-world _as the placement path for placeables_; drag stays for in-grid rearrangement, and drop-via-`[x]` stays for everything. Per-item placement is delegated to a `PlaceableSpec` registered alongside the item def: `{ verb: 'set' | 'set up' | 'sow' | 'lay' | 'place'; canPlace(state, x, y): boolean; place(state, x, y, uid): void }`. Meteorite spec appends to `state.placedMeteorites` and decrements the equipped stack; camera spec instantiates a `PlacedCamera` and consumes the slot entirely. HUD affordance: a slot on the permacomputer bottom-bar instrument cluster (`RP-28`) shows the in-hand item's glyph in hot pink with its name. The cursor's hot-pink highlight tile is unchanged in form; when something is in hand and the hovered tile passes `canPlace`, the cursor's inverted-dark foreground glyph swaps to the in-hand item's glyph — a "loaded" cursor reading as the steward's hand pre-empting the tile. When `canPlace` is false, the loaded glyph is suppressed (the cursor reads as its usual highlight, no payload), so legality is readable at a glance. Stack semantics: in-hand is sticky, each place consumes one, the stack decrements, in-hand auto-clears when empty. Right-click (or a keybinding deferred to spec) unequips. Net cost: one field, one registry, one input branch, one bottom-bar slot, a label sweep. Small-to-medium.

> **Calla:** The drag was twitchy and the verb was wrong; this fixes both at once without making the steward give anything up. Drop stays — there are weeks when the inventory is messy and the steward just needs to put a thing down — and place arrives as a distinct gesture for the moments that matter. Two verbs is more truthful than one. The HUD affordance is what makes the second verb legible: a slot on the permacomputer bottom bar shows what is in hand right now, glyph and name, in the same hot pink the world preview uses. The steward glances down and sees what they are _carrying with intent_, separate from what is in the bag. _That_ is what the drag-and-drop tried to express and could not. The bag holds; the hand declares — and the cursor confirms. The cursor's form stays the hot-pink tile the steward already recognizes; the only change is that the tile's inner glyph becomes the in-hand item when the steward is hovering a legal placement. A small, quiet affordance — not a new cursor mode, just a hint that the next click will mean something. When the hovered tile is illegal, the loaded glyph drops out; the cursor returns to its plain highlight, and the steward learns the boundary without a message. Concerns the spec needs to honor: an explicit unequip path so a hair-trigger left-click on a long walk does not surprise the steward; `canPlace` honest about which tiles are legal; per-item hover-hint copy that reads from the verb — _set down_, _set up_, _sow_ — and not from a uniform substrate.

> **Delta:** The frame to break is the word "equip." It is the right mechanic and the wrong noun — it carries an RPG inheritance the prairie is otherwise free of (no class, no level, no slot tree). The prairie's word is _in hand_. The steward has the meteorite _in hand_; the steward _takes the camera in hand_; the manual reads "to set down what is in hand, click." Small change, just labels — but labels are what the steward sees, and the prairie's language is half the game. The deeper frame, and this is the line worth locking, is that this round is the moment the inventory stops being a container and becomes a relationship. The grid is not a backpack; the grid is everything the steward is currently carrying _and currently meaning_. The bottom-bar slot is the second sentence of the same paragraph — the bag holds; the hand declares. Drop did not need to die for place to be born; the two verbs need each other. One open thing the round should name and not solve: when this lands, `RP-11`'s drop-on-dirt-to-plant becomes _sow-from-in-hand_, and the seed-into-soil contact gets the same treatment. That is not for this spec; that is the second-order tax this idea pays the rest of the inventory. _Two verbs is what makes one of them a ceremony._

### Consensus

- New field on `GameState`: `equippedItemUid: ItemUid | null`. Single-owner write through the inventory layer, registered in `EXPECTED_FIELDS`, uid-keyed for stack/sort/merge resilience.
- Left-click (currently unused outside cursor positioning) becomes the place action when something is in hand. Nothing-in-hand left-click behavior is preserved.
- `[x] to drop` is preserved untouched. Drop remains a general inventory affordance for every item type, placeables included. Place is _additive_, not a replacement — the steward has two verbs available.
- Drag stays for in-grid rearrangement. Drag-from-grid-to-world is retired as the _placement_ path for placeable items; drop via `[x]` remains for those items.
- Each placeable item registers a `PlaceableSpec` — `{ verb, canPlace, place }`. Hover hints and in-game prose read the item's verb.
- In-hand is sticky: each place consumes one; the stack decrements; in-hand auto-clears when empty. An explicit unequip path is required (right-click or a small keybinding, deferred to spec).
- The cursor's hot-pink highlight tile is unchanged in form. When something is in hand and the hovered tile passes `canPlace`, the cursor's inner glyph reads as the in-hand item — a "loaded" cursor. When `canPlace` is false, the loaded glyph drops out; the cursor returns to its plain highlight. Hot-pink-as-user-action stays locked.
- Permacomputer HUD affordance: a slot on the bottom-bar instrument cluster (`RP-28`) shows the in-hand item's glyph and name in hot pink. The bag holds; the hand declares.
- The noun in player-facing copy is _in hand_, not _equipped_. Code can keep `equippedItemUid` for engineering clarity; manual entries, hover hints, and prose read "in hand," "take in hand," "set down."
- **Amendment to v4 R7:** _the inventory is the character sheet_ now has its mechanic. The in-hand-and-place layer is the seam where that doctrine becomes a verb instead of a metaphor.

### Tracked as

## Round 3: elevation has an opinion now

_Tyler: "i think this feature is a matter of increasing the height possible during genesis and introducing a 'walkable' threshold level. the rub is going to be balancing it out so that either all tiles are topographically accessible OR accepting that some mesas, for example, might not be accessible."_

> **Astrid:** Then the question is older than the spec. The prairie has always had places the eye can hold that the feet cannot — distant escarpments in the Loess Hills, the bluffs above the Mississippi, an Ozark glade catching late-afternoon light across a draw you cannot cross before dark. That is not a defect of the landscape; that is the landscape having _shape_. The cosmology's answer to Tyler's rub is direct: _the prairie does not owe the steward access._ A mesa visible but unreachable is the prairie expressing its own scale, the same way the year is unreachable in compressed form. Force-carving ramps until every tile is connected would be the same impulse as the boot animation Round 7 of v4 banned — the consumer-electronics gesture of guaranteeing the steward gets what they see. Refuse it. Some places are seen and not walked. _What you cannot reach, you keep more carefully._ The threshold itself becomes a register the steward learns: a single step up is a stride, two is a scramble the boots refuse. The cosmology has held thresholds before; this is one more.

> **Boon:** Substrate is already in the tree. `state.elevation: Map<string, number>` exists today (`src/engine/genesis/state.ts:55`, hoisted onto `GameState` at `src/engine/state.ts:311`), is uplifted during genesis on a 0–100 scale, and is read cosmetically by `getElevationLift` (`src/engine/projection.ts:210`) and weighted into the lightning weight (`src/engine/lightning.ts:128-129`). It is not yet read by `movement.ts`. The work, in order: (1) turn up the genesis amplitude on `UpliftEpoch` so the existing field starts producing structurally meaningful peaks instead of cosmetic bumps; (2) add an `isClimbableStep(state, fromKey, toKey, threshold)` predicate that returns false when `|elev(to) - elev(from)| > threshold`; (3) gate `src/engine/movement.ts:106` on _both_ `isWalkableTile` _and_ `isClimbableStep`; (4) run a post-genesis connectivity pass from the spawn tile, mark the resulting set as the _reachable mass_, leave the rest as the unreachable cohort — no carving, no edits, just bookkeeping; (5) `state.reachableMass: Set<string>` registered in `EXPECTED_FIELDS`, used by ruin placement, character spawning, and anything else that wants to guarantee its target sits in the reachable cohort. Render side: no new tile type, no new glyph, no new pass — the iso lift is already doing the 3D for free, and v11 R2 locked one visual language. The v7-round x-ray idea becomes an alpha rule in `tileBgComposite.ts`: if a tile's lift would occlude the avatar's bounding box, drop its alpha. That is a small render change, not a new pass. The cost of this whole spec is one new field, one new predicate, one connectivity pass, one branch in movement, and an alpha rule. Small-to-medium. The expensive part is _tuning_ the amplitude and threshold so the prairie produces shapes the eye reads as bluffs and mesas instead of noisy hills.

> **Calla:** When the steward walks toward a step they cannot take, they need to _know_ before they collide with it — otherwise the threshold reads as random ("I climbed that one, why not this one?"). The lift already gives a visual cue, but the system needs a second one: a dark cliff-face line drawn on the iso side wall between two tiles whose delta exceeds the threshold. That is the cliff. Not a tile, not a glyph — a shadow on the side of the rise. The renderer already draws lifted tiles; this is the line at their base where the lift becomes uncrossable. Click-to-move pathfinding has to honor `isClimbableStep` or the steward will queue a path that strands them halfway up a bluff. And the "minor procedural cliffs" of the v7 round need a craft check: a three-tile pimple sticking up in the middle of an otherwise walkable field is the frustrating shape; a long ridge the steward can walk _along_ until they find the saddle that lets them cross is the satisfying one. The amplitude should produce ridges and mesas, not warts. Astrid's _seen and not walked_ is a beautiful sentence when the unreachable thing is the distant bluff with the lone tree on top; it is just annoying when it is a 2x2 island in a clearing. Tune the noise toward _landmark scale_.

> **Delta:** The frame to break is the word "cliffs." The room has been talking about cliffs since v7, and Tyler just renamed the feature without saying it. There are no cliffs. There is _elevation that finally matters_. The cliff is what the steward calls the place where the elevation field has an opinion strong enough that the feet have to listen. Same field that has been sitting in `state.elevation` all along — same lift, same lightning weighting — just turned up far enough that movement notices. This is the round to lock the framing: **elevation is the substrate, the threshold is the verb, the cliff is the noun the steward applies when they bump into it.** Rename in the manual: not "cliff," but "rise" or "scarp" or "the bluff" — whatever the species of landform is. And lock the second thing the cosmology has already answered: _the reachable mass is not the whole map._ A spawn-connected reachable cohort, an unreachable cohort, no carving. ### Consensus

- **No new tile type, no new glyph.** RP-41 is implemented by extending the existing `state.elevation: Map<string, number>` field — turn up the genesis amplitude on `UpliftEpoch` so peaks are structurally meaningful instead of cosmetic — and gating movement on a per-step elevation delta threshold. **Amends the v7 round's framing of RP-41**, which treated cliffs as a new impassable tile type.
- **Walkability rule.** Add `isClimbableStep(state, fromKey, toKey, threshold)` returning false when `|elev(to) - elev(from)| > threshold`. Gate `src/engine/movement.ts:106` on both `isWalkableTile` _and_ `isClimbableStep`. Click-to-move pathfinding honors the same predicate.
- **Reachability is not guaranteed.** Post-genesis, walk a connectivity pass from the spawn tile to compute `state.reachableMass: Set<string>`. No carving, no ramp-stamping — tiles outside the mass remain part of the prairie, visible from afar, unwalkable. _The prairie does not owe the steward access._ Ruin placement, character spawning, and any other system that requires the steward be able to reach its target reads `reachableMass` as a precondition.
- **New `GameState` field: `reachableMass: Set<string>`.** Single-owner write at genesis, registered in `EXPECTED_FIELDS`. Recomputed if RP-44 winter geology lifts/drops elevation.
- **The cliff is a shadow, not a tile.** Renderer adds a dark cliff-face line drawn on the iso side wall between adjacent tiles whose elevation delta exceeds the threshold. No new pass — extension to `tileBgComposite.ts` or its sibling, consistent with the one-visual-language lock from v11 R2.
- **X-ray, revised.** The v7 round's x-ray idea survives but is no longer a cliff-tile property. It becomes an alpha rule in the render side: any tile whose iso lift would occlude the avatar's bounding box drops its alpha. Applies to all lifted tiles, not just cliffs.
- **Amplitude tuning targets _landmark scale_.** The noise should produce ridges the steward can walk along until they find a saddle, and mesas large enough that "I can see it but cannot reach it" reads as scale, not as a 2x2 wart in a clearing. Three-tile pimples are the failure mode; bluffs and ridgelines are the success mode. Tuning happens at spec time against the existing genesis seed corpus.
- **No new naming.** The manual continues to use "cliff" where the steward bumps into one, because that is the word the steward will use. Internally, the system is _elevation + threshold_; the cliff is the noun applied at the moment of refusal.
- **Cave and ruin entrances on rises** (the v7-round arches) are still in scope but become a placement rule, not a tile-type interaction: the entrance is a normal `zoneTransition` tile placed somewhere along an unwalkable face. Defer the geometry to spec.

### Tracked as

## Round 4: the prairie's own evidence

_Tyler: "let's actually change the way reviewing camera film functions. right now it is stubbed in with incomplete behavior. brainstorm ways we can make this make sense with an ascii game. i don't know what to do. i was thinking about having text summaries explaining the photo but that's kinda not fun / doesn't make sense. i was thinking of drawing ascii art of the actual plants? but then i get tired thinking about sourcing all those. what is the point of the camera? what does it help the player accomplish or decide?"_

> **Astrid:** The question Tyler added is the question the round needs to answer: what is the camera _for_? It is not a tool. It is not RPG inventory. It is the prairie's evidence of its own slowing — the thing that holds a fragment of the world while the steward is somewhere else. The five lines name this directly: _the lineage is the medium_, and the camera is one of the few objects in the prairie capable of producing lineage. A photograph in this cosmology is not a trophy and not a record-of-mine — it is a witness, a thing that says _this happened here, while you were not._ The gel is the wrong register for that work; it speaks the scan instrument's language, abstract and decoded, decoupled from the soil it came out of. The photograph wants to speak the prairie's own language: the same iso the steward already reads, the same glyphs, the same colors, only held still and held smaller, with the grain of time on it. And — this is the cosmology speaking — the camera does not notice _events_. The camera notices _change_. Pollination is a category the camera does not know; bloom is a category the camera does not know. What the camera knows is that the cells in its footprint were one configuration and are now another. A roll of film over a patch that did not change yields one frame, and that one frame is true: _nothing happened here, and the camera was watching._ A roll over a patch that bloomed and was visited and was eaten yields many, and the spacing between them is the duration of the prairie's attention. The contact sheet is the natural shape — the roll is a single artifact made of moments where the prairie was no longer what it had been. _A photograph in this prairie is the prairie's own evidence of itself, and the roll is the prairie's evidence of having been more than one thing._

> **Boon:** The substrate audit makes both the capture-model shift and the playback shift cheaper than they look. `captureCells` at `src/engine/timeLapse.ts:103` already snapshots the rendered `{ char, color }` of all nine cells of the 3x3 footprint, in the same register `renderer.ts` uses for the world (tile base + flora overlay + bee/monarch overlay; the comment at line 49 names what is intentionally elided — cursor, fog, seasonal wash, path). Those cells live on every `TimeLapseFrame`. The capture trigger flips: instead of `recordCameraSubjectEvent` being called from seven or eight site-specific hooks (pollination, rain front, bloom, ember, monarch, ghost passage, egregore advance, character approach), a single hook in the sim loop — same cadence as flora lifecycle advancement (`src/engine/flora/spread.ts`) — calls `captureIfChanged(state, camera, now)`. Compute the current 9-cell state, compare against the last frame on the roll, append iff any cell differs. The eight call sites delete. The `subject: CameraSubject` field on `TimeLapseFrame` retires; the `subjectLabel` switch and the heading register in `TimeLapsePlayback.tsx:27-50` retire with it. (`CameraSubject` may live on if other systems use it — the scan instrument or a future spec — a small audit decides; if no one else uses it, the enum deletes too.) A baseline frame is captured at camera placement, so every subsequent diff has something real to diff against. Film count keys to captured changes one-to-one — one detected change consumes one film unit, which is the natural read and ties `RP-15` wear tighter than the event model ever did. Playback becomes a contact sheet: a CSS grid of N frames laid out chronologically, each rendered as a small iso-projected 9-cell photograph using the same iso transform `renderer.ts` uses, just at a reduced cell-pitch, framed with the existing `film-grain-overlay` class. Click-to-expand on a single frame is the held-read affordance — small modal over the contact sheet, cross-fade between adjacent frames if the steward steps; the contact sheet itself does not animate. `GelBandView` exits `TimeLapsePlayback.tsx` and stays alive in the codebase for the scan instrument, where it belongs. Defer the exact heuristic to spec. Net code surface: smaller after than before. _The cheapest spec is the one that retires fields and call sites instead of populating them._

> **Calla:** The session-shape this unlocks is the one the camera was always reaching for. The steward picks up the camera, opens the roll, and sees the contact sheet — every moment the patch noticed change, laid out at once. A dead-soil camera with one frame says one thing: _I watched, and nothing happened._ A bloom-monarch-death camera with twelve frames says another: _look what you missed._ Both are legible at a glance, and both are honest in a way the old flipbook was not — the steward was being shown a sequence as if each frame was equally weighted, when in fact some patches had a lot to say and others did not. The contact sheet lets the prairie's variance be visible. Reading the sheet is the contemplative act: your eye traces the chronology, finds the frame where the clover became decay, sits with it. If a single frame wants a held read, click expands it; the rest of the sheet stays peripherally visible behind. The vocabulary is the same the steward already reads in the world, only held still — same iso, same glyphs, same colors, the only addition is the grain of time on the predecessor frames. Predecessor footage from `RP-24` works _better_ in this model than in the flipbook — the steward will encounter cameras they did not place, and the sparseness or density of the contact sheet tells them, before they read a single frame, what kind of place that was and how loudly its tenure rang. _The vocabulary is the same; only the tense is different — and now the rhythm is too._

> **Delta:** The frame to break is "the camera records events." The camera does not know what an event is. The camera knows what change is. Drop the subject taxonomy from the camera's mind entirely — `CameraSubject`, `recordCameraSubjectEvent`, the seven or eight site-specific hooks that were the camera trying to be a journalist, narrating what kind of thing it just saw. The camera is not a journalist; the camera is a witness, and a witness does not categorize. The witness notices that the world is no longer what it was, and records that. That is the whole job. The contact sheet is the natural shape because the roll is one artifact, not a sequence — you do not flip through someone's diary one page at a time. You spread it on the table and read it as a whole, and the moments you linger on are the moments _you_ choose. _The camera is patience, and patience is what you read in the spacing between frames._ One frame is a patient soil; twelve frames is a soil that had a year. The prairie's variance becomes a register the steward learns to read. Two practical fallouts. First — the camera captures a baseline frame the moment it is placed, so every subsequent frame is a diff against something real. The first frame is the camera saying _here is what was here when I started looking_. Second — the camera stays silent, as the consensus below already locks. No projector hum, no shutter click, no celebratory chime when a frame is recorded. The prairie does not announce itself; the camera does not either. _A photograph here is the prairie remembering on the steward's behalf, and the roll is the prairie's whole memory of one patch over one tenure._

### Consensus

- **The camera's purpose, named.** The camera is the prairie's evidence of moments the steward missed. It witnesses on behalf of the absent observer. Reviewing the roll is the steward reading what the prairie noticed while they were elsewhere.
- **The camera records change, not events.** Capture is diff-driven: a single hook in the sim loop computes the current 9-cell state, compares against the last frame on the roll, and appends a new frame iff any cell differs. `recordCameraSubjectEvent` and its seven or eight call sites retire.
- **The `CameraSubject` enum and the `subject` field on `TimeLapseFrame` retire from the camera pipeline.** `subjectLabel` and the heading switch in `TimeLapsePlayback.tsx:27-50` retire with them. If no other system uses `CameraSubject`, the enum deletes; audit at spec time.
- **A baseline frame is captured at camera placement.** Every subsequent frame is a diff against something real. The first frame says _here is what was here when I started looking._
- **Capture cadence matches flora lifecycle advancement.** One sim-loop hook, same heartbeat the prairie already advances on.
- **The photograph is the captured 3x3, iso-projected.** Playback renders the nine `TimeLapseCell` values using the same iso transform `renderer.ts` uses for the world, at a smaller cell-pitch, framed with the existing `film-grain-overlay` class.
- **The roll is a contact sheet.** Playback is a grid of all frames at once, chronological left-to-right, top-to-bottom, with timestamps under each. Variance is itself the read — a dead-patch roll shows one photo; a lively-patch roll shows many.

- **The gel-band view is removed from primary playback.** `GelBandView` stays in the codebase for the scan instrument's use, where it belongs.
- **The camera stays silent.** No instrument voice added to review.
- **Annotation is rejected for this round.** No species names, no "what's here" overlay. The photographs speak.
- **Predecessor footage from `RP-24` shares this pipeline.** Degradation is a `degradation` prop (`{ grain, tint, glyphLeak }`) on the photograph component, applied per-frame in the contact sheet. Density of the sheet itself signals tenure liveliness before the steward reads any single frame.

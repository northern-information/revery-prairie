# Precis thinktank — v10

A continuation of v9. v9 ran 2 rounds, locking the Knot's plural witness set (steward, Emily, coyote, egregore-via-Gron) and the domestic-vs-wild time pause (cellar and little house pause `state.timeOfDay` and `state.season`; cave and ruins do not). v10 opens with a question Tyler brought to the room on 2026-05-23: _what do you think about adding a night phase? i sort of want to add fireflies and will-o'-the-wisp._ The room found that v9 R2's forward reference to `state.timeOfDay` had been waiting for this — the daylight clock has been the unbuilt half of the temporal cosmology — but that a wall-clock-driven day cycle would tick faster than the seasons it lives inside (the year already collapses into twenty minutes; five minutes per season; `SEASONAL_PHASE_PERIOD_MS`), and that Revery is the once-per-year solstice event, not a nightly sleep loop. The reframe was holographic: each season holds one day and one night. Four days, four nights, per year.

What follows is the rounds. Decisions are stated as decisions. Open questions are flagged. v10 is _additive_ to v9 — it does not invalidate v9 unless a round here explicitly amends it. Items that should propagate back to earlier versions are tagged `**Amendment to v{N}:**`.

## Round 1: four nights, holographically

_Tyler: "what do you think about adding a night phase? i sort of want to add fireflies and will-o'-the-wisp."_

_(Tyler corrected the room mid-round: the daylight clock cannot be wall-clock seconds because the year already collapses into twenty minutes; and Revery is the solstice event, not a nightly sleep. The round restarted from that ground.)_

> **Astrid:** The reframe is the round. The prairie does not live in days; it lives in seasons. A full year cycles every twenty minutes of wall-clock time, five minutes per season — there is no diurnal scale beneath that without breaking the substrate. So the answer is not _add a day/night clock_ but _let each season hold one day and one night_. Four days, four nights, per year. The night is not a unit of time; it is the prairie's _gesture_ of darkness at each seasonal scale. v2 R5's cosmology — _the ecology that wakes when ours sleeps_ — has always been a seasonal claim, and what Tyler is asking for is the same claim rendered at higher resolution: the egregoric ecology wakes not just in winter, but in the _night_ of every season, briefly, four times a year. The cosmology is more consistent for this, not less. _Heat death is the antagonist; night is the antagonist's daily face, made small and ritualized into four passes._ Each seasonal night has its own character — the spring night is the prairie waking and fireflies emerging; the summer night is short and warm; the autumn night is the shoulder where the egregoric script reads sharpest (v7 R4's lock); the winter night is long, bone-cold, and the diurnal-by-seasonal compounding makes it the year's crispest reading of the of-but-not-of script. _The night is a register the season borrows for a minute or two, and gives back._

> **Boon:** Substrate, corrected. No new wall-clock counter. The night/day phase is _derived_ from `seasonalPhase` — one new pure function, `dayPhase(seasonalPhase): { phase: 'day' | 'night' | 'dawn' | 'dusk', t: number }`. Within each season's `[k/4, (k+1)/4)` span on the year-circle, a `nightFraction(season)` table allocates a portion to night and the rest to day — long winter nights, short summer nights, intermediate spring and autumn. The shoulder bands (dawn, dusk) are thin transitions on either side. At 5 minutes per season with, say, a 40/60 day/night split in winter and 70/30 in summer, the winter night is ~2 minutes long and the summer night is ~90 seconds. The render side is a global tint wash composing with the existing `seasonalWash(seasonalPhase)` in `filmGrainOverlay.ts` — multiply the night tint over the seasonal tint, both derived from the same scalar. No new clock, no new field on `GameState`, no save-shape change. v9 R2's `pausesPlayerTime` flag still pauses `seasonalPhase` advancement, which now correctly pauses _both_ the season clock and the derived day/night phase in one stroke — the holographic reading earns its keep at exactly that seam. The gaze radius compresses during the seasonal night; the recent #429 doubling holds during day, clips back toward the pre-#429 value during night. Fireflies: still a sparse particle pass keyed to tall grass and damp tiles, emitting during spring and summer nights only. Wisps: drifting entities whose nightly path encodes the prior _year's_ footprint of one of v9 R1's plural witnesses (corrected — a wisp per seasonal night is not a wisp per day; the recurrence rate is four per year, and the footprint they encode is the same-season slice of the previous year). Net LOC: tiny for `#52`, small for `#53`, moderate for `#54`. No clock; just a derivation.

> **Calla:** This is better. Four guaranteed nights per year is a _beat_, not a routine. The steward cannot opt out — there is no nightly sleep to skip a night with; the solstice Revery is the only sleep, and it advances the whole year past the very night it would have skipped. So when the spring night arrives at, say, the 1/8 mark of the year, the steward _will_ witness it. It is not a thing they negotiate with; it is a thing the prairie does. That makes each of the four nights its own scene — the steward learns the spring night's character, the summer night's character, and so on, the way they have already learned the seasons. The little house glowing at night still holds — windows lit, Emily inside, lamp on the porch — because the steward will be _somewhere_ when night falls, and the homestead-as-beacon means the steward who happens to be far from home when night begins can navigate by it. The fact that nights are brief (90 seconds to a couple of minutes each) is exactly right for tone: the night is a _passage_, not a phase. The session texture becomes: tend, walk, dusk arrives, the palette cools, the fireflies emerge or do not (depending on season), maybe a wisp appears, the steward chooses to follow or not, dawn arrives, tend resumes. Four times a year. _The night is small enough to be a guest and large enough to be felt._

> **Delta:** _The prairie does not have nights. The year has four._ That is the line, and it is the line because it changes what kind of cosmological object a night is. It is not a daily fact dressed up; it is a quarterly _event_, a thing the cosmology gives the steward on a schedule the cosmology controls. Four nights a year means each one is _watched_ — the steward will remember the autumn night of year 3, the way they remember a thunderstorm. The wisps fit this exactly: at one wisp per seasonal night, you get up to four wisps a year — the right tempo. A wisp is supposed to be uncommon, supposed to be a _summons_. Hourly wisps would have been wallpaper; four-a-year wisps are events. The witness encoding from the previous round still holds: a wisp on the spring night of year 4 walks the spring footprint of one of year 3's witnesses — the coyote, the egregore, or Emily. The steward who follows is walking a same-season-prior-year witness-trail in real time. The cellar's prose, years later, says _the coyote walked the northwest ridge in the spring of year 3_; the wisp showed the steward the trail in spring of year 4, untexted. _Same data, two surfaces — the cellar speaks across years, the wisp speaks across one._ Fireflies stay native and seasonal: present in spring and summer nights, absent in autumn and winter. Real-world phenology. The night the fireflies do not come is its own kind of cold.

## Round 2: price it, don't monetize it

_Tyler: "how do we make money off of this?" — "i've been making free and open source software my entire life. all my music is free. i feel like we have something here that is authentic, novel, and meaningful. i want it to hit hard. i think if people pay for it then it will hit harder. this isn't about me getting rich; it's about perceived value. i want to honor how much time and energy i've put into this thing."_

> **Astrid:** The room must not relitigate the v4 R5 and R7 locks — _permanence is a capitalist assumption_, _the verb that resists entropy is tending_, _that is a verb capitalism cannot monetize, which is precisely why the game's politics work_. None of that bends. But Tyler has identified the real seam, and the seam is honest: the artifact and the verb are not the same thing. A book has a price; reading it is unpriced. A record has a price; listening to it is unpriced. The cosmology of the prairie says _tending cannot be commodified_; it does not say _the object that contains tending cannot have a price_. v7 R1 made the analogous move for genetics — the steward observes, names, hangs the Knot; the steward is not the selector. That is the verb-side discipline. Now the artifact side: the prairie is a small true book the player crosses a threshold to enter. The threshold has a price; what happens inside does not. _The price is the threshold; the tending is free._ Wendell Berry's books cost money. Rachel Carson took royalties. Ursula K. Le Guin sold paperbacks. The politics live in the work, not in the absence of a price. Pricing the artifact is consistent with the cosmology; refusing to price it because the cosmology is anti-capitalist would be the contradiction the room must avoid — it would treat _free_ as if free were not itself a market posture, which it is.

> **Boon:** Substrate. There are three distribution surfaces worth taking seriously. itch.io takes a sliding cut, accepts DRM-free uploads of arbitrary build shapes, and rewards small artisanal releases — it is the surface Tyler's FOSS lineage maps to most cleanly. Steam takes a flat 30% cut, has the largest discovery surface for desktop games, and requires a desktop wrapper (Tauri or Electron — the canvas + React renderer ports cleanly to either, with Tauri preferred for footprint). The current Cloudflare workers + pages hosting can keep a bounded free demo running indefinitely at near-zero marginal cost. So: itch + the existing web stay live; Steam is added when the game is feature-complete; web hosts the demo, itch hosts the full DRM-free download, Steam mirrors the same. Multiplayer is the only real ongoing infra cost — the worker plus Durable Objects scale with active player count, and a one-time purchase does not naturally cover recurring infra. Two options: (a) treat multiplayer as a free bonus on the purchased game, gated by an ownership token that the worker validates before accepting a connection, and amortize the worker cost across sales; (b) ship single-player canonical and let multiplayer remain free-with-purchase but explicitly degrade-to-LAN if the worker goes down. Strongly prefer (a) for launch; revisit if the worker becomes load-bearing. License-token plumbing is small: a per-purchase HMAC token issued by the storefront on first launch, included in the worker handshake (`shared/src/protocol.ts`), validated worker-side. Single-player has no token check — DRM-free means the desktop build runs without the network at all. Net engineering work for monetization: token plumbing in `shared/`, a handshake check in `worker/src/`, a demo-boundary check at the second-year solstice in `src/engine/`, a desktop wrapper. Modest scope.

> **Calla:** The session texture changes when you pay. A free download is _picked up_; a paid game is _committed to_. Tyler's instinct on perceived value is right and it is not vanity — it is exactly the disposition the cosmology wants in its players. The steward who paid the threshold price is already further into the right posture: they have committed before they have entered, which is the same shape as the tenure itself. _Price reinforces the loop, not the other way around._ But the three feelings of monetization must stay separated and not get conflated, because the failure modes of each are different. One: _the threshold of arrival_ — does the price filter the audience toward the right disposition. Hard yes here; a small, honest, whole-number price is the right shape. Two: _the texture of ownership_ — does the player feel they own the thing they bought, or are they renting permission. Hard yes on ownership; DRM-free download, lifetime updates, no phone-home, no kill switch, no "license expired." Three: _the loop of post-purchase_ — does the game ask for more money after the purchase. Hard no, every direction. No DLC, no microtransactions, no battle pass, no cosmetic store, no subscription, no patron tier that gates content, no "deluxe edition," no expansion that locks the original artifact behind a paywall. The artifact is bought once and tended forever, which is structurally identical to the tenure. Also: do not do pay-what-you-want on the main artifact. PWYW asks the player to negotiate the artifact's value at the moment of acquisition, which makes the artifact feel uncertain about its own worth. Set a price. Honor it. The soundtrack on Bandcamp can be name-your-price — that is Tyler's existing free-music practice and belongs there, not on the game. A printed companion later — the steward's field guide as a real object — can have its own price and lives outside this round.

> **Delta:** _Price it. Don't monetize it._ That is the round. Tyler has been arguing with himself about whether selling the prairie betrays his FOSS lineage, and the room has been arguing about whether the anti-capitalist physics survive a price tag, and both arguments are using the wrong word. _Pricing_ is what a publisher does for a book — names the artifact, sets a number that says "this is enough." _Monetization_ is what a platform does for attention — extracts ongoing value from an audience the artifact has caught. They are different acts, with different verbs, different audiences, different timescales. The verb capitalism cannot monetize is _tending_; that lock holds. But pricing an object and monetizing a population are not the same operation, and conflating them is what makes artists in Tyler's lineage refuse to ever sell anything — and the refusal, paradoxically, is itself a market posture, because _free_ in 2026 means "discoverable by algorithms in exchange for telemetry." Free is the capitalist move at scale; it is the move that needs ten million strangers to feel important. Selling is the small move — the one that says _i made one true thing, here is its price, this is enough_. There is no contradiction. The anti-capitalist physics live inside the artifact, where tending happens. The price lives at the threshold, where the audience self-selects. Tyler's FOSS lineage is honored, not abandoned, by treating this artifact differently — because the lineage's whole point was always to refuse extraction, not to refuse honoring the work. _A free game needs an audience. A priced one needs a reader._ Pick the reader.

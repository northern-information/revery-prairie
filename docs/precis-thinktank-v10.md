## Round 1: four nights, holographically

_Tyler: "what do you think about adding a night phase? i sort of want to add fireflies and will-o'-the-wisp."_

_(Tyler corrected the room mid-round: the daylight clock cannot be wall-clock seconds because the year already collapses into twenty minutes; and Revery is the solstice event, not a nightly sleep. The round restarted from that ground.)_

> **Astrid:** The reframe is the round. The prairie does not live in days; it lives in seasons. A full year cycles every twenty minutes of wall-clock time, five minutes per season — there is no diurnal scale beneath that without breaking the substrate. So the answer is not _add a day/night clock_ but _let each season hold one day and one night_. Four days, four nights, per year. The night is not a unit of time; it is the prairie's _gesture_ of darkness at each seasonal scale. v2 R5's cosmology — _the ecology that wakes when ours sleeps_ — has always been a seasonal claim, and what Tyler is asking for is the same claim rendered at higher resolution: the egregoric ecology wakes not just in winter, but in the _night_ of every season, briefly, four times a year. The cosmology is more consistent for this, not less. _Heat death is the antagonist; night is the antagonist's daily face, made small and ritualized into four passes._ Each seasonal night has its own character — the spring night is the prairie waking and fireflies emerging; the summer night is short and warm; the autumn night is the shoulder where the egregoric script reads sharpest (v7 R4's lock); the winter night is long, bone-cold, and the diurnal-by-seasonal compounding makes it the year's crispest reading of the of-but-not-of script. _The night is a register the season borrows for a minute or two, and gives back._

> **Boon:** Substrate, corrected. No new wall-clock counter. The night/day phase is _derived_ from `seasonalPhase` — one new pure function, `dayPhase(seasonalPhase): { phase: 'day' | 'night' | 'dawn' | 'dusk', t: number }`. Within each season's `[k/4, (k+1)/4)` span on the year-circle, a `nightFraction(season)` table allocates a portion to night and the rest to day — long winter nights, short summer nights, intermediate spring and autumn. The shoulder bands (dawn, dusk) are thin transitions on either side. At 5 minutes per season with, say, a 40/60 day/night split in winter and 70/30 in summer, the winter night is ~2 minutes long and the summer night is ~90 seconds. The render side is a global tint wash composing with the existing `seasonalWash(seasonalPhase)` in `filmGrainOverlay.ts` — multiply the night tint over the seasonal tint, both derived from the same scalar. No new clock, no new field on `GameState`, no save-shape change. v9 R2's `pausesPlayerTime` flag still pauses `seasonalPhase` advancement, which now correctly pauses _both_ the season clock and the derived day/night phase in one stroke — the holographic reading earns its keep at exactly that seam. The gaze radius compresses during the seasonal night; the recent #429 doubling holds during day, clips back toward the pre-#429 value during night. Fireflies: still a sparse particle pass keyed to tall grass and damp tiles, emitting during spring and summer nights only. Wisps: drifting entities whose nightly path encodes the prior _year's_ footprint of one of v9 R1's plural witnesses (corrected — a wisp per seasonal night is not a wisp per day; the recurrence rate is four per year, and the footprint they encode is the same-season slice of the previous year). Net LOC: tiny for `#52`, small for `#53`, moderate for `#54`. No clock; just a derivation.

> **Calla:** This is better. Four guaranteed nights per year is a _beat_, not a routine. The steward cannot opt out — there is no nightly sleep to skip a night with; the solstice Revery is the only sleep, and it advances the whole year past the very night it would have skipped. So when the spring night arrives at, say, the 1/8 mark of the year, the steward _will_ witness it. It is not a thing they negotiate with; it is a thing the prairie does. That makes each of the four nights its own scene — the steward learns the spring night's character, the summer night's character, and so on, the way they have already learned the seasons. The little house glowing at night still holds — windows lit, Emily inside, lamp on the porch — because the steward will be _somewhere_ when night falls, and the homestead-as-beacon means the steward who happens to be far from home when night begins can navigate by it. The fact that nights are brief (90 seconds to a couple of minutes each) is exactly right for tone: the night is a _passage_, not a phase. The session texture becomes: tend, walk, dusk arrives, the palette cools, the fireflies emerge or do not (depending on season), maybe a wisp appears, the steward chooses to follow or not, dawn arrives, tend resumes. Four times a year. _The night is small enough to be a guest and large enough to be felt._

> **Delta:** _The prairie does not have nights. The year has four._ That is the line, and it is the line because it changes what kind of cosmological object a night is. It is not a daily fact dressed up; it is a quarterly _event_, a thing the cosmology gives the steward on a schedule the cosmology controls. Four nights a year means each one is _watched_ — the steward will remember the autumn night of year 3, the way they remember a thunderstorm. The wisps fit this exactly: at one wisp per seasonal night, you get up to four wisps a year — the right tempo. A wisp is supposed to be uncommon, supposed to be a _summons_. Hourly wisps would have been wallpaper; four-a-year wisps are events. The witness encoding from the previous round still holds: a wisp on the spring night of year 4 walks the spring footprint of one of year 3's witnesses — the coyote, the egregore, or Emily. The steward who follows is walking a same-season-prior-year witness-trail in real time. The cellar's prose, years later, says _the coyote walked the northwest ridge in the spring of year 3_; the wisp showed the steward the trail in spring of year 4, untexted. _Same data, two surfaces — the cellar speaks across years, the wisp speaks across one._ Fireflies stay native and seasonal: present in spring and summer nights, absent in autumn and winter. Real-world phenology. The night the fireflies do not come is its own kind of cold.

### Consensus

- Days and nights are **holographic at the seasonal scale**. The year cycles every twenty minutes (`SEASONAL_PHASE_PERIOD_MS`), five minutes per season; each season holds one day and one night. Four days, four nights, per year.
- No new wall-clock timer. The day/night phase is **derived from `seasonalPhase`** via a pure function — `dayPhase(seasonalPhase)` returning `{ phase: 'day' | 'dawn' | 'night' | 'dusk', t: number }`. No new field on `GameState`, no save-shape change.
- A `nightFraction(season)` table allocates the night/day split per season. Lean: long winter nights, short summer nights, intermediate spring and autumn — encoding real-world phenology. Exact numbers deferred to `#52` spec.
- The night palette is a global tint wash composing with the existing `seasonalWash(seasonalPhase)` in `filmGrainOverlay.ts`. Multiply the night tint over the seasonal tint; both derive from the same scalar. Composes with v7 R2's two-layer fog — night dims the present layer, not the memory layer.
- The steward's gaze radius compresses during the seasonal night. The #429 doubling holds during day; night clips back toward the pre-#429 radius. Exact numbers deferred.
- The seasonal night cannot be opted out of. There is no nightly sleep — Revery is the once-per-year solstice event (`REVERY_COOLDOWN_MS = SEASONAL_PHASE_PERIOD_MS`). The four nights are guaranteed beats the steward will witness. The little house's `pausesPlayerTime` flag from v9 R2 pauses both the season clock and the derived day/night phase in one stroke — domestic structures stop the seasonal night the same way they stop everything else.
- The little house renders as a beacon during the seasonal night — windows lit, Emily inside, lamp on the porch. The steward can navigate to home from anywhere in the prairie by aiming at the warm rectangle.
- The egregore script-bearing tiles read sharpest at the **diurnal-by-seasonal compounding** — the winter night, in particular, is the year's crispest reading of the of-but-not-of script. v2 R5's cosmology now holds at both scales.
- **Fireflies are native** — of-this-Earth bioluminescence. Present during spring and summer nights only. Absent in autumn and winter. Real-world phenology. _The night the fireflies do not come is its own kind of cold._
- **Will-o'-the-wisps are not fireflies.** A wisp on the seasonal night of year N encodes the same-season footprint of one of v9 R1's plural witnesses from year N-1 — coyote trail, egregore patch perimeter, Emily's housebound circuit. The steward who follows a wisp is walking a witness-trail from one year ago. The cellar reads back the same path in prose years later; the wisp is the live, untexted, one-year-delayed sibling.
- Up to four wisps per year — one per seasonal night. The wisp is a _summons_, not wallpaper. Hourly wisps would have been ambience; quarterly wisps are events.
- The two lights stay sharply distinct in code, in render, and in lore. Conflating them would be the same category mistake as letting the egregore wear a coat.
- _The prairie does not have nights. The year has four._

### Open questions deferred to specs

- (Boon) `nightFraction(season)` table values. Lean: winter ~0.55, autumn ~0.45, spring ~0.40, summer ~0.30 — long winter nights and short summer nights, calibrated for tone not realism. Defer to `#52` spec and playtesting. The constraint: the total night per year should be small enough that the four nights feel like _events_, not like a quarter of the session.
- (Astrid) Whether dawn and dusk are their own palette frames or just interpolated transitions. Lean: their own register — the shoulder minutes of each seasonal night are where the egregoric script is most legible (the diurnal analog of v7 R4's shoulder-season lock). A small alpine-glow tint that flatters the script for a few seconds around each transition. Defer to `#52`.
- (Calla) Whether the steward can carry a light source (lantern, torch) to widen the night gaze. Lean: no, and never as a craftable item. The compressed gaze is part of the night's texture; arming the steward against it converts the night from a register to a problem. If a light source ever enters, it belongs to a wisp the steward chose to follow.
- (Delta) Which witness footprint feeds the wisp on a given seasonal night, and how the choice is made. Lean: deterministic from year and season — the spring-night-of-year-N wisp encodes the spring slice of year-N-1's most prominent witness footprint (whichever covered the most tiles in that season). If no witness covered the season meaningfully, no wisp appears that night. The cosmological constraint, unchanged: never the steward's own footprint.
- (Astrid) Whether wisps have a sound. Lean: a near-silence that the player learns to hear (calm-tech register, v4 R9). The wind sounds different where a wisp passes. No howl, no chime. Defer to spec.
- (Calla) Whether the first seasonal night of a new tenure introduces itself somehow — a slight pause, a longer dusk, a tutorial-soft moment. Lean: no — the prairie does not narrate itself. The new steward learns the seasonal night by being inside one.
- (Delta) Whether the wisp can be "lost" — what happens if the steward stops following, or walks away. Lean: the wisp continues its path regardless; it is not the steward's wisp. It dissolves when its path ends, on schedule, whether or not the steward watched. Defer to `#54`.
- (Astrid) Lore voice for manual entries on fireflies and wisps — fireflies in the native voice, wisps in the of-but-not-of register. Authoring is human-only per `docs/claude/manual.md`. Flag for human lore author at `#53` and `#54` spec time.
- (Boon) Egregore visibility during winter night — does the script-bearing tile render get an explicit boost, or does the layered tint math (winter wash + night tint) already produce the contrast Astrid is naming? Lean: the layered math gets you most of it; audit during `#52` spec, only add an explicit boost if the contrast isn't crisp enough.

# Backlog thinktank — v5

A continuation of v4. v4 ran rounds 0–9, locked the cosmology's five lines, named the diegetic test, named the bottom bar as the permacomputer, and named permacomputing.net as source doctrine. v5 picks up after v4 with a new arc, sparked by a question Tyler brought to the room on 2026-05-21 while specifying `#28`: _which way does the weathervane point?_ The room found that the question was the wrong size. The real question underneath was: _what is north?_

What follows is the rounds. Decisions are stated as decisions. Open questions are flagged. v5 is _additive_ to v4 — it does not invalidate v4 unless a round here explicitly amends it. Items that should propagate back to `docs/backlog-thinktank-v4.md` are tagged `**Amendment to v4:**`.

Carried forward from v2/v3/v4 with no changes:

## Open and locked

**Locked in v5:**

- **The diamond is the world.** The iso projection is not a viewing layer applied to a flat grid; it is the shape of the world the steward inhabits. World `(x, y)` storage indices are internal storage, not a coordinate frame the game exposes.
- **The cardinal frame rotates 45° to align with the diamond.** N is the top point of the diamond. E is the right. S is the bottom. W is the left. The ordinals (NE / SE / SW / NW) align with the storage axes themselves.
- **No coordinate translation lives anywhere in the game.** The display layer does not translate between two frames because there is only one frame.
- **The compass points at the world the steward inhabits, not at the grid the storage uses.** Portable principle for any future surface that reports a direction or a position.
- **Amendment to v4 round 9 diegetic test:** the test extends to coordinate frames. _Is this coordinate frame readable from the world the steward inhabits?_ A coordinate frame that does not match the world is a voice in geometry's clothing.
- **The unreadable does not announce its source.** No in-game text refers to Voynich as an external object. The script exists in the prairie because the prairie produced it; the manual does not name a source because the steward cannot name what they cannot read. Sibling to the compass test — _is this reference readable from the world the steward inhabits?_
- **The EVA-token generator samples phonemes, not transcribed MS 408 text.** Wire-layer invariant. If a future change ever pulls strings from real folio transcriptions, direct exposure to a Voynich solve stops being zero.

## Round 1: the diamond is the world

_Tyler: "we're committed to iso projection. what does that mean for cardinal directions? the genesis system used ortho projection for NESW. i have a gut feeling we actually want the tip of the diamond at the very north of the map to become north — and the very southern tip to become the south pole — instead of the northwest and southeast corners."_

> **Boon:** Before anyone reacts, the code already half-said yes to this. `worldDeltaToIsoPx` in `src/engine/projection.ts:74` projects world `(x, y)` onto screen as `((x − y) * w, (x + y) * h/2)`. The world's `(0, 0)` corner lands at the **top tip** of the diamond on screen. The `(max, max)` corner lands at the **bottom tip**. World-`(+x, 0)` runs down the **right edge**; world-`(0, +y)` runs down the **left edge**. The current `WindDirection` enum calls the direction toward `(0, −y)` "north" — but on screen, that is the **upper-right edge** of the diamond, not the top point. The compass and the projection have been silently 45° out of phase for the entire history of the codebase. `tileWater.ts:47` has a function called `windToFrontAxis` that sweeps rain "from the north" along world-`y`, which crosses the screen as a diagonal — not top-to-bottom. The substrate has been lying about cardinals. Tyler's gut is the cleanup.

> **Astrid:** And once the cleanup is named, it is enormous. The diegetic test from round 9 was about _instruments not lying about what is observable._ This is the same rule applied one layer deeper: _the coordinate frame itself does not lie about what is observable._ The world the steward inhabits is the diamond. The diamond has a top point and a bottom point. To call the top _northwest corner_ is to import a viewing frame the steward does not have. **North is the top of the diamond.** South is the bottom. East is the right. West is the left. The labels finally match the world. _It is not a rotation; it is a confession._

> **Astrid:** And genesis is not hypothetical — it is already broken in exactly this way. `src/engine/genesis.ts:1444` says _"Glaciers advance from top and bottom"_ in a comment, and the code under it measures `topDist = y − SPACE_BORDER` and `bottomDist = sim.height − SPACE_BORDER − y`. Under the current substrate, that iteration advances along storage-`y`, which on screen is _the upper-right edge to the lower-left edge_ of the diamond — a diagonal sweep. The design intent was always _from the north pole and the south pole_, which under the rotated frame is _from the top tip and the bottom tip of the diamond_. The code wrote the intent in English and then walked a different geometry. The doctrine names what the code already wanted to say: **icecaps advance from the poles, the poles are the diamond's tips, and the iteration measures distance from those tips.**

> **Calla:** And the player payoff lands at the level of geological feeling, not just the vane. _Ice caps emerge from the top and bottom points of the diamond._ That sentence is what the design has always wanted to be true; the code wrote it in a comment and then did something else. After `#30`, the icecaps actually arrive from where the steward would point at them. Rain fronts sweep across the diamond in the direction the cardinal names. Future latitude-banded features — equatorial blooms, polar fauna, anything keyed to north-south — place themselves where their names say they go. The doctrine and the picture become one picture. _The compass and the world finally rhyme._

> **Delta:** The line worth naming: _the diamond is the world._ Not _the diamond is the projection of the world._ Not _the world is a square the camera tilts._ **The diamond is the world.** Iso is not a viewing choice we apply to a flat grid; iso is the world's shape. The `(x, y)` storage indices are exactly that — storage indices, the way bytes on disk are storage indices for a string. The grid is internal. The diamond is the world. _The compass points at the world, not at the storage._

> **Boon:** Concretely, the change is two layers. **Layer one — the labels rotate.** `WindDirection`'s enum keeps its eight keys (`N`, `NE`, `S`, …), but each key's semantic meaning rotates 45°: `WindDirection.N` denotes the diamond's top tip, which in storage is the `(−x, −y)` direction. The `WIND_SCREEN_VECTORS` table in `src/engine/weather/wind.ts:32` is rewritten so each cardinal's `(sx, sy)` matches the new mapping. `windToFrontAxis` in `src/engine/tileWater.ts:47` and the pollen-bias logic in `src/engine/effects.ts:47-53` follow. **Layer two — the iterations rotate.** Four sites in `src/engine/genesis.ts` (lines 1451, 1563, 1594, 1983) measure `topDist`/`bottomDist` along storage-`y`. Under the rotated frame, polar distance is `u = x + y` from the top pole and `2·(height − 2·SPACE_BORDER) − u` from the bottom pole. The glacial edge noise at line 1438, currently indexed by `x` (one per column), reindexes by the perpendicular coordinate `v = x − y`. Same shape of math, rotated 45° at the source. Roughly five engine files plus tests; the migration is one PR.

> **Calla:** The 19th-century farmer reading from round 9 holds, too — actually it sharpens. The vane on a real barn reports the direction of the wind in the world the farmer stands in. The farmer's world has a north pole. _So does the steward's._ The prairie has a top tip and a bottom tip; those tips are the poles. The vane on the permacomputer reports the wind in the world the steward is in. Compass-truth and screen-truth become _the same truth_ — that was the deeper version of Tyler's iso request from earlier. **The two frames collapse to one.** The vane points where it points. The letter beside it agrees. The pollen blows the way the arrow says.

> **Delta:** Which dissolves the question `#28` started with. There was never an iso-vs-world choice to make; the world _is_ iso. The question only seemed real because the labels had drifted out of phase with the geometry. The fix is not to translate at the display layer. The fix is to retire the translation by making the labels honest. **No coordinate translation lives anywhere in the game after this round.** That is the doctrine.

> **Astrid:** And the cosmology gets a sixth line, or near it. The five lines from round 7 — heat death, tending, tenure, lineage, inventory — are about the game's stakes and verbs. _The diamond is the world_ is about the game's space. It sits next to them as a structural statement, not above them. **The compass points at the world the steward inhabits, not at the grid the storage uses.** That is the principle. It is portable to anything that reports a position or a direction — the minimap, the sidebar cursor, a future predecessor record that says _your mother's grave is in the north of the prairie._

### Consensus

- **The diamond is the world.** The iso projection is not a viewing layer applied to a flat grid; it is the shape of the world the steward inhabits. The world's `(x, y)` storage indices are internal storage, not a coordinate frame the game exposes.
- **The cardinal frame rotates 45° to align with the diamond.** N is the top point of the diamond. E is the right. S is the bottom. W is the left. The ordinals (NE / SE / SW / NW) align with the storage axes themselves (NE = down the right edge of the diamond / storage-`+x`; SE = down the lower edge / storage-`+y`; etc.).
- **No coordinate translation lives anywhere in the game.** The display layer does not translate between two frames because there is only one frame.
- **Both the labels and the iterations rotate.** `WindDirection`'s `(sx, sy)` table and downstream mappings rotate. The four `topDist`/`bottomDist` sites in `src/engine/genesis.ts` (1451, 1563, 1594, 1983) rewrite to measure polar distance along the rotated frame — `u = x + y` from the top pole, `2·(height − 2·SPACE_BORDER) − u` from the bottom pole. Glacial edge noise reindexes from `x` to the perpendicular coordinate `v = x − y`.
- **Icecaps now advance from the diamond's top and bottom points.** The comment at `genesis.ts:1444` finally describes what the code does. Future latitude-banded features land where their names say they go.
- **`#28` ships under the new frame.** The vane arrow and letter are derived from the rotated cardinals directly; both agree by construction. Wind-speed labels lock as `still / breeze / brisk / gusty / gale`. Threshold values defer to spec. `#28` depends on `#30`.
- **Doctrinal addition:** _the compass points at the world the steward inhabits, not at the grid the storage uses._ Goes under **Locked in v5**. Portable to all future surfaces that report a direction or a position.
- **Amendment to v4 round 9:** the diegetic test now extends to coordinate frames — _is this coordinate frame readable from the world the steward inhabits?_ A coordinate frame that does not match the world is a voice in geometry's clothing.

## Round 2: if the manuscript gets solved

_Tyler: "what's our risk exposure if the voynich manuscript every gets solved?"_

> **Boon:** Start with what's actually on the wire after the in-flight change, because the answer turns on it. The worktree that swaps `EGREGORE_GLYPHS` to the Voynich PUA (`U+F120..U+F15F`) and drops the Latin pierces from tile rendering moves us from _looks-like-Voynich-via-fallback_ to _is-Voynich-via-the-kreativekorp-font_. After it lands, the tile glyphs are real Voynich code points; the `'Voynich'` `@font-face` in `src/styles/index.css` finally maps something. But the _strings_ those glyphs form are still `EVA_TOKENS` — procedurally sampled phonemes that look Voynich-shaped, not transcribed from any folio of Beinecke MS 408. **Direct exposure to a Voynich solve is still effectively zero,** but the reason is different: the substrate now uses the manuscript's script, not the manuscript's text. A decipherment of MS 408 yields a key from glyphs to meaning. Our tiles run those glyphs through a procedural generator that was never trained on the folios. The key would unlock our script and find sampled noise underneath. _Right alphabet, wrong language._ Manual entries are the same story — pierces stay in the manual bodies (`LATIN_PIERCE_WORDS`), tile rendering drops them, but neither change touches the EVA-token generator that produces the strings being decoded.

> **Astrid:** And yet the in-flight change _raises_ the associative exposure even as it leaves the direct exposure at zero. Before the swap, the player was reading Latin-ish substitutes that gestured at the manuscript. After the swap, the player is reading **the manuscript's actual glyphs.** The cultural valence sharpens. The texture is no longer _evocative of_; it _is_. If MS 408 is solved tomorrow, the script the player sees on egregore tiles will be — for the slice of the audience who follows manuscript news — _the recently-decoded script_. That is a different aesthetic register than _the eternally unreadable_. The phenomenology of _this refuses me_ still works locally, because the strings remain procedurally meaningless. But the cultural halo we are renting is now visibly the manuscript's halo, not a near-copy. _The lift gets bigger and the borrowed thing gets more specific in the same motion._

> **Calla:** The player who sits with an egregore tile is reading prose that won't resolve. After the in-flight change, the prose is even more committed to that — Tyler dropped the Latin pierces from tile rendering precisely because the substitutes were goofy and broke the immersion. _The script refuses me_ is now uninterrupted on the tile face. That phenomenology is local: it comes from the tile, the font, the manual page, and the readable surroundings. A solved Voynich does not change any of that for a player who never knew Voynich existed. For the player who _does_ know, the register shifts — and after the swap it shifts more, because what they recognize is now exactly the script, not a Latin neighbor. But it shifts toward _something else still strange_, not toward _ordinary script_. The unreadable on the page is still unreadable on the page. _The script does not care whether its referent has been decoded._

> **Delta:** The line worth naming: _we are not running on Voynich. We are running on the gesture Voynich points at._ The gesture is "writing that does not yield." That gesture predates Beinecke MS 408 by a thousand years and survives it. Linear A is partially decipherable now; the Phaistos Disc is not; the rongorongo glyphs are not. There is always _an_ unreadable text in the human record. The cosmic-horror register the game enters does not depend on _which_ unreadable text is current. It depends on the gesture being available, and the gesture is structurally available — because there is always something written that nobody can read. Even after the glyph swap, what we are riding is _the gesture_, not _the citation_. The PUA migration just makes the gesture more legible to the kind of player who would recognize it. _The substrate is the gesture, not the citation._

> **Boon:** Practical hardening, if we want any, is at the naming layer and at the string layer, not the rendering layer. Dev surfaces say "Voynich" in places they don't have to: `src/styles/index.css` `@font-face` family, `docs/claude/egregores.md`, `tools/fetch-voynich-font.mjs`. After the in-flight change, _the file path_ `/fonts/voynich.ttf` also names it. None of these surface to the player; the manual category tab is derived from `EGREGORE_GLYPHS.slice(0, 4).join('')`, the tile type is `TileType.Egregore`, and no in-game string contains the word "Voynich." **Contamination is entirely in dev surfaces.** A future solve would not cause any player-facing rename. The principle: _Voynich is the referent we used to think about the gesture during construction, not a name we expose to the world the steward inhabits._ Code-side renames can defer; the value is grep hygiene, not exposure mitigation. The one wire-layer hardening that _does_ matter is the EVA-token generator — confirm at spec time it samples phonemes, not transcribed folios. If it ever pulls from real MS 408 text, direct exposure stops being zero.

> **Astrid:** And the deeper protection is doctrinal, not lexical. The egregore vocabulary was locked in v3 precisely to keep the player out of the "is this _real_ Voynich" frame. We banned the word "invasive" because it was the wrong cosmology. We can extend the same instinct: the in-world name for the script is whatever the manual calls it, which is _nothing_ — it has no in-world name, because the steward cannot name what they cannot read. _The unreadable does not announce its source._ A reader who solves Voynich and comes to the game looking for the same key finds no lock — there is no lock, there is only a texture using the manuscript's letters to spell nothing in particular. **The game does not claim to be using the manuscript. It is using the experience of encountering one, with the manuscript's letters as the texture.**

> **Calla:** And the round produces a small constructive thing, not just an absence of risk: it confirms what we should _not_ build. We should not write a manual entry that gestures at the manuscript as a real-world artifact. We should not have an NPC say "the script of the lost book." We should not let any predecessor record include a citation. The doctrine becomes a constraint going forward: **no in-game text refers to Voynich as an external object.** The script exists in the prairie because the prairie produced it. That is the cosmology. The moment a manual entry calls the script "the manuscript's script," the game has stepped outside itself, and the steward has stepped outside the diamond. _The compass test from round 1 generalizes: is this reference readable from the world the steward inhabits?_

> **Delta:** Which gives v5 its second portable principle, sibling to "the diamond is the world." Round 1 said the coordinate frame must be readable from the world the steward inhabits. Round 2 says **the references must be readable from the world the steward inhabits, too.** No external citation may pierce the diegesis. Voynich is the construction-time referent for a texture the prairie generates — and after the in-flight swap, it is also the literal source of the glyphs. The texture stays. The referent never enters the world. _Risk exposure: aesthetic, low, asymptotic to zero as we stop saying Voynich out loud in places the player might read it — and as the EVA generator stays uncontaminated by real folio text._

### Consensus

- **Direct exposure to a Voynich solve is effectively zero, even after the in-flight glyph swap.** The worktree that moves `EGREGORE_GLYPHS` to the Voynich PUA (`U+F120..U+F15F`) and drops Latin pierces from tile rendering uses the manuscript's _script_ but not the manuscript's _text_. EVA tokens are procedurally sampled phonemes; pierces in manual bodies (`LATIN_PIERCE_WORDS`) are sixteen English cosmology words. A decipherment of MS 408 produces a key our strings were never written with.
- **Associative exposure rises with the glyph swap, but remains bounded.** The cultural valence of seeing the manuscript's actual glyphs is sharper than seeing Latin-ish substitutes. For the slice of the audience that follows manuscript news, the register shifts on a hypothetical solve from _eternally unreadable_ to _recently decoded_. The phenomenology of _this script refuses me_ remains local to the tile, the font, the manual page, and the surrounding readable text.
- **The substrate is the gesture, not the citation.** The unreadable text in the human record changes (Linear A → partially solved; Phaistos / rongorongo → still opaque). The gesture _writing that does not yield_ is permanent. The game rides the gesture; the in-flight swap makes the gesture more legible without changing what the game is running on.
- **Doctrinal addition for v5 locks:** _no in-game text refers to Voynich as an external object._ Sibling to round 1's compass test. The script exists in the prairie because the prairie produced it; the manual does not name a source because the steward cannot name what they cannot read.
- **Construction-time naming is dev-surface only.** `src/styles/index.css` `@font-face` family, `docs/claude/egregores.md`, `tools/fetch-voynich-font.mjs`, the `/fonts/voynich.ttf` path, and dev comments use the word "Voynich." No player-facing string does today. Hardening at the naming layer is grep hygiene, not exposure mitigation — defer-able indefinitely.
- **One wire-layer invariant worth pinning at spec time:** the EVA-token generator samples phonemes, not transcribed MS 408 text. If a future change ever pulls strings from real folio transcriptions, direct exposure stops being zero. Track as a checkable property going forward.
- **What we should not build:** a manual entry that gestures at the manuscript as a real-world artifact; an NPC line that names "the script of the lost book"; a predecessor record that cites a source. Negative space, locked.
